# "A day on the block" questions (Module 3), ready to edit

Edit anything below: the situation, the answer choices, which one is best, and the explanations. Add or delete situations if you like. When you're done, ask Claude to copy your edits into `training/index.html`.

Each situation has **3 answer choices**. Mark the best one with **(BEST)**. Each choice has an explanation that people see after they pick it.

---

## Situation 1

**Situation:** You get to your first house. The app asks, “Could you observe the property?” A high fence is blocking most of the front.

- **A.** Tick yes and fill in what you can't see using what's typical on the block.
  - *Explanation:* Every box you tick has to be something you saw. Guessing from the rest of the block puts made-up data into the predictive model.
- **B. (BEST)** Pick “View blocked.”
  - *Explanation:* “I couldn't see it” is real data. It tells the i-Team the house needs another visit.
- **C.** Skip this one and don't record anything.
  - *Explanation:* A skipped house looks the same as one nobody visited. Recording that you couldn't see it is more useful.

## Situation 2

**Situation:** You can see the house now. Time to register your location.

- **A. (BEST)** Tap Register location now, while you're standing in front of it, or fill in the address.
  - *Explanation:* Either one works. The location is how the City knows which house your notes are about.
- **B.** Tap Register location on the bus on your way home, when you have better signal.
  - *Explanation:* Register location stamps wherever you are when you tap it, so tapping it on the bus attaches your notes to the wrong place. If you can't register at the house, fill in the address instead.
- **C.** Reuse the location from the last house, since it's on the same block.
  - *Explanation:* Two houses on a block can be in very different shape. A location that's off by one house attaches your notes to the wrong address.

## Situation 3

**Situation:** A woman on the next stoop calls out: “What are you doing taking pictures of houses?”

- **A.** “City business, ma'am,” and keep working.
  - *Explanation:* Brushing people off is how rumors start. Neighbors have every right to ask.
- **B. (BEST)** Stop. Say hi and your name, show your ID, and say you're volunteers with the City. Explain the project in detail if she asks, stressing that you are just engaged in data collection.
  - *Explanation:* Be friendly and honest. Residents get scam calls from fake “City” people all the time, so showing your ID matters. Making clear you're only collecting data, not deciding anything about anyone's home, helps people feel at ease.
- **C.** “We're checking which houses are vacant. This one's probably getting flagged.”
  - *Explanation:* Never say a house is “flagged,” “condemned” or “getting a VBN.” It isn't true, and it can scare people or help scammers.

## Situation 4

**Situation:** She warms up: “That one's been empty since Miss Dorothy passed. Her grandson still pays the water, though.”

- **A. (BEST)** Tick “A neighbor told me this property is vacant,” and write what she said and how she knows (she lives next door).
  - *Explanation:* Neighbor reports go last on the form, with your notes on how the person would know. The detail about the water bill is useful too, because it shows why the predictive model's water data can miss a house like this.
- **B.** Tick vacant, and also tick “People going in or out” to be safe.
  - *Explanation:* Only tick what you or she actually reported. Adding things “to be safe” makes the data less safe.
- **C.** Ignore it. Neighbors gossip.
  - *Explanation:* Neighbors often know more than anyone. Record it, say where it came from, and let the City weigh it.

## Situation 5

**Situation:** You go around back. The alley has twelve backs that all look the same.

- **A. (BEST)** Count houses from the corner, the same way you did out front, and check the color. Put both in the app next to your back photos.
  - *Explanation:* Field testers found this was the hardest part of the survey. Counting from the corner on both sides is the fix.
- **B.** Photograph whichever back looks most vacant.
  - *Explanation:* That's how you end up recording the wrong house, and it's guessing toward a result.
- **C.** Ask a guy in the alley which one it is and skip the count.
  - *Explanation:* He might be right, but he might not. Count for yourself, and you can note what he said.

## Situation 6

**Situation:** From the alley you see someone climb out of the basement window of your house.

- **A.** Walk over and ask what they're doing.
  - *Explanation:* You're not security and you're not code enforcement. Approaching puts you and them at risk.
- **B. (BEST)** Keep your distance and don't take their picture. Stick to recording the building's condition. If it feels off, leave and tell your field lead.
  - *Explanation:* The survey is about the building, not the people around it. And your safety matters more than any record. “Unsafe to approach” is always a valid answer.
- **C.** Call out to them and take a photo as evidence.
  - *Explanation:* Never photograph people and never confront anyone. You're collecting data, not evidence.

---

## Situation 7

**Situation:** Scoring time. It's between Moderate and Severe. Your partner says, “Just put Severe so the City takes it seriously.”

- **A.** Put Severe. Your partner has done more of these.
  - *Explanation:* A higher score doesn't get a house more attention. The score isn't a vote, and nudging it makes the data worse.
- **B. (BEST)** Pick the level whose description fits best, and say why in your comments. You and your partner can score differently.
  - *Explanation:* The i-Team sometimes sends two people to the same house on purpose, to check how consistent the training is. Honest disagreement is useful.
- **C.** Leave the score blank since you're not sure.
  - *Explanation:* A best guess with a reason in your comments is more useful than a blank.

---

## Situation 8

**Situation:** Lunch. Your group chat wants pics of “the creepiest house you saw today.”

- **A.** Post one but crop out the address.
  - *Explanation:* People recognize their own block, and it's someone's grandmother's house. Even without the address, it's identifiable.
- **B. (BEST)** Don't share it. Survey photos stay in the app.
  - *Explanation:* Photos, addresses and notes are City data about real families. They go to the i-Team and nowhere else. That includes AI tools.
- **C.** Send it as a DM only.
  - *Explanation:* A DM gets screenshotted just as easily. Same answer.

---

## End-of-module summary ("The norms, in short")

People see this list after the last situation. Edit it to match your changes.

- Record only what you saw. “Couldn't see it” is a real answer.
- Register your location at the house, or fill in the address instead.
- Be friendly and honest with residents. Show your ID, make clear you're only collecting data, and never predict what will happen to a house.
- Safety first. Never approach anyone, and leave if it feels off.
- Your score is your own honest read.
- Survey data stays in the app.
